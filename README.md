# The Likeness of Humanity in AI: 09-30-2026

---------------------------------------------

There was an interesting article written by a Murat Durmus on LinkedIn. Normally, LinkedIn 
content has too much market flair, and not enough substantive depth. Likewise, this article 
by Durmus did not go into depth, but it did serve to point out one of the problems in 
discussion of A.I. development. 


Here is a passage from this article. 

> AI alignment has an awkward question: aligned with which us?  
> Somewhere right now, an engineer is testing whether an AI model will lie to protect itself.  
> Down the hall, a colleague is rehearsing the story they’ll tell their manager about why the project is late.  

We want machines to be honest but kind, helpful but not flattering, obedient but willing to refuse. They should understand what we meant, not just what we said.

Every time. No bad days.

Now look at the species writing that list.

We say we want the truth, then punish people for telling us. We value our health, but apparently not after 11 p.m. We want things to be fair, with a few reasonable exceptions involving ourselves.

If a human were an AI system, the audit would be brutal.

Inconsistent goals. Easily manipulated. Changes principles when hungry.

Not ready to deploy.
